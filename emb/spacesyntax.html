3Settings

Axial map: the fewest and longest straight lines of sight that cross every space and join into one network (found from the wall corners). Integration shows the lines most connected to all others; choice, the through-routes. Uses the walls of the plan.

m

Smaller gives more, finer lines; larger, only the main ones.

m

Segment angular analysis: street center lines broken at every junction; a turn of 90° costs one step. NAIN (integration) finds the centers people go to, NACH (choice) the streets people go through. 800 m is about a 10-minute walk; try 2000–5000 m for the city scale. Draw center lines with the Street tool or import a DXF and set its road layer to Street.

m

Visibility graph analysis (VGA): a grid of nodes over the floor, joined wherever two nodes see each other. Integration says how few visual steps it takes to reach everywhere else from a node; connectivity, how much it sees. At knee height furniture blocks, so the graph follows how people can walk.

After the analysis: agents pick a spot in their 170° view, walk 3 steps toward it and pick again (as in DepthmapX). Their counts show where movement gathers.
