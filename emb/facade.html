How this works

Box counting, after Carl Bovill's Fractal Geometry in Architecture and Design (1996). The elevation is drawn as lines at 512 px wide and covered with grids of boxes 2 to 64 px across. The fractal dimension D is the slope of the log–log line: how fast the number of boxes that touch a line grows as the boxes shrink. D near 1 means plain lines; closer to 2 means detail at every scale. Upload a street elevation, a photo of the neighbours or a figure-ground plan, then press Match: the tool tries values of the type's main density setting (levels for Fractal relief) and keeps the one whose D is closest. Use it to argue that a new facade sits in its context, not as proof.
