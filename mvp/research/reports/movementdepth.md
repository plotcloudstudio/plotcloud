# Abstract

Space syntax research distinguishes "natural movement", the part of pedestrian flow that is generated by the configuration of space itself, from movement drawn by attractors [1, 2]. Turner and Penn showed that simple agents that walk towards randomly chosen visible points, on a visibility graph, produce gate counts that correlate with observed movement in a gallery about as well as visual integration does [3]. SimuSite's Movement + depth study runs such agents on the visibility graph of each floor and combines their crossings with visual integration into one index, shown on a blue–white–pink scale in the manner of DepthmapX [4]. This report documents the agent model and the combination. The agent rules and integration follow the published methods (see the Space syntax report for the graph and integration). The combination, 0.65 times the square root of the normalised gate count plus 0.35 times normalised integration, is a presentation choice of SimuSite with no published basis; it should be read as a ranking of places within a plan.

# Body

## 1. Introduction

Hillier and colleagues found that, in urban grids, the integration of street segments explains much of the variation in pedestrian counts, and called this natural movement [1]. Inside buildings, Turner and Penn's exosomatic visual architecture (EVA) agents, which pick destinations among the points they can see, reproduce aggregate movement patterns in the Tate Britain galleries with correlations similar to those of integration [3]. Agent gate counts and integration are therefore two estimates of the same configurational tendency, one from a stochastic walk and one from graph depth.

## 2. Method

The study builds the visibility graph of the chosen level as described in the Space syntax report (nodes inside the walls, joined where mutually visible at eye or knee height) and computes visual integration [HH] [5]. It then runs 200 agents for 1,500 steps. Each agent starts at a random node with a random heading; it chooses a destination among nodes visible from its current node, at least two grid spacings away and within ±85° of its heading (after 30 failed attempts the field-of-view rule is relaxed), walks three grid steps towards it, and chooses again. Agents that would leave the floor turn around. Each time an agent enters a new node, the node's gate count increases by one [3, 4]. A fixed random seed makes runs repeatable.

The combined index at node k is

I_k = 0.65 √(clamp((g_k − g₁)/(g₉₉ − g₁), 0, 1)) + 0.35 clamp((i_k − i₁)/(i₉₉ − i₁), 0, 1),

where g is the gate count, i the integration, and the subscripts 1 and 99 their 1st and 99th percentiles. The study shows the index, integration and gate counts as separate views, and plays back the walks of the first 48 agents.

## 4. Verification and validation status

The Workspace tests check that the agents walk and that the view and replay controls work. The agent rules were checked against the description of Turner and Penn [3]. Gate counts have not been compared with DepthmapX's agent analysis on the same plan, nor with observed movement. In the end-to-end audit of 20 November 2026, the study ran on level 1 of the audit block (644 nodes at 60 cm) and recorded 2,122 crossings at the busiest node.

## 5. Discussion

The two components are well founded, but the index that combines them is not a published measure. The weights favour the agents; the square root makes moderately used areas stand out more than a linear scale would. Different weights would change which mid-ranked areas appear pink. For decisions, the separate views of integration and gate counts are easier to interpret and to defend with the literature [3, 5].

Agent results depend on the number of agents and steps: with 200 agents and 1,500 steps on a few hundred nodes, counts are reasonably stable for the busiest areas but noisy in rarely visited corners. Agents have no destinations, purposes or knowledge of the building; real occupants walk to lifts, desks and exits. The study shows the movement that the layout alone encourages, which is useful for placing shared functions, displays or seating, but it does not forecast how many people will pass a point. The Everyday circulation study models purposeful trips between rooms.

## 6. Conclusion

The agent model and integration used by the Movement + depth study are sourced and implemented as published; the combined index has weights chosen for presentation rather than from evidence, and it should be read as a relative ranking. Reporting the two components alongside the index, as the study does, keeps the result traceable.
